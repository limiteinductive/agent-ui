A Field Notebook You’ll Actually Use

What to write down on a hike while there’s still mud on your boots.

Mara Chen
September 17, 2026

Last spring I came home from a hike with 47 photos and no idea where I’d taken the one I cared about. It showed a stream crossing just below a fork. I’d wanted to tell a friend how to get there, but the trail looked the same in every frame. The next weekend I tucked a pocket notebook into my jacket and wrote down the first thing I knew I’d forget.

Write at the next place you stop

I take notes when I’m already stopping to drink, check the map, or pull on a layer. At a junction, I’ll jot down what the sign actually says. At a crossing, I’ll describe where I stepped in and what the far bank looked like. A ten-second note is usually enough.

Use whatever landmark you can pick out again. “Creek after the second footbridge” is more useful on the walk back than “creek at 11:42.” A time stamp earns its place when I want to match a note to a photograph or remember how long a climb took.

Write the detail you’d want someone to tell you at the trailhead.

On a foggy morning in September, that detail was a pair of cairns about thirty paces apart. The first marked the turn, and the second was far enough down the path that I couldn’t see it from the junction. I wrote “turn at first cairn, look for second” and was glad to have it on the way back.

Give each walk a name you can find later

While dinner is on, I copy the few lines I want to keep into a Markdown file. The filename combines the date and route, like 2026-09-12-ridge-loop.md. Inside, the first heading is the name I’d use when telling a friend about the walk. I keep the paper notebook for rough sketches and anything I haven’t made sense of yet.

Once I had a few dozen files, I made a small index. This Python script reads the first level-one heading from each dated note and prints a Markdown list with the newest walks first. Run it from the directory that contains your notes folder.

from pathlib import Path

notes = Path("notes").glob("????-??-??-*.md")

for path in sorted(notes, reverse=True):
    date = path.stem[:10]
    lines = path.read_text(encoding="utf-8").splitlines()
    title = next(
        (line[2:].strip() for line in lines if line.startswith("# ")),
        path.stem[11:].replace("-", " ").title(),
    )
    print(f"- {date} · [{title}]({path.as_posix()})")

I keep the index next to the notes folder so the links resolve when I open it in my editor. Before repeating a route, I skim the last entry for things worth checking again, such as a seasonal water source or a trail sign that was down.

Leave yourself one reason to come back

I try to end each entry with something I’d like to see on a return trip. On the ridge loop it was the old fire road that branched off toward the coast. The fog was thick enough that I could only see the first hundred yards. A month later I went back on a clear morning, took the turn, and finally saw where it led.
